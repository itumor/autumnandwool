Merino or lambswool: which should you choose?

Merino and lambswool are both soft, warm wool fibres, and both are used in our knitwear. They differ in where the fibre comes from and how the yarn feels. This guide explains the difference in general terms, then shows which of our pieces use each fibre.

What merino is

Merino is a breed of sheep, and merino wool is the fleece from those sheep. Merino fibres are generally finer than the wool of many other breeds, so merino knits often feel smooth against the skin. It is a common choice for next-to-skin layers.

What lambswool is

Lambswool is the fleece from young sheep, usually taken at their first shearing. Because the fibres come from young animals, they are generally softer and more springy than wool from adult sheep. Lambswool knits tend to feel light and full of loft.

Feel, fineness and drape

Merino is usually the finer of the two, so it tends to feel close and smooth. Lambswool has more loft, so a lambswool knit can feel springier and fuller. Both fibres drape well. The stitch and the yarn weight matter as much as the fibre, so read the description on each product page.

Warmth and weight

Neither fibre is automatically warmer. Warmth depends on the thickness of the yarn and how tightly the piece is knitted. A fine merino knit can feel as warm as a heavier lambswool piece if the yarn is dense. Compare the material line and the fit notes before you decide.

Care

Both need the same gentle care. Wash by hand in cool water with a mild wool detergent, do not wring, and dry flat. Our guide on how to wash a wool sweater covers each step.

The fibres in our range

Our merino pieces list merino wool on their product pages, and our lambswool pieces list 100% lambswool, which is also pure new wool. For example, the Lightweight Merino Turtleneck is 100% merino wool, and the Women's Classic Lambswool Crew Sweater is 100% lambswool. Check the material line on the product you are looking at, because it is the source of truth.

Which one to choose

Choose merino if you want a finer, smoother feel. Choose lambswool if you want a softer, springier knit. Both are good everyday choices, so start with the style you like, then check the fibre. You can browse the full range on our wool sweaters page.
