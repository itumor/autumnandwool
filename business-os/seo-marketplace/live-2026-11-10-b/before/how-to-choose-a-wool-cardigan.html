How to choose a wool cardigan

A cardigan can be worn most days, but the styles differ. Some are long and open, some have a shawl collar, and some have raglan sleeves. This guide helps you choose by style, length and fibre, and it points to the cardigans in our range.

Start with the style

Our cardigans come in three styles. The long open-front cardigans have a shawl or draped collar, and one is knee-length. The open-front cardigan is straight-cut and has ribbed side slits. The merino raglan cardigan has raglan sleeves and comes in black, brown or blue.

Open front or shawl collar

A shawl collar drapes softly at the neck and front. The open-front cardigan with side slits is straight-cut, so it has a simpler line. Both are 100% pure new wool, as their product pages state, so the choice comes down to the shape you prefer.

Raglan for an easy shape

A raglan sleeve runs from the underarm up to the neck, which gives a relaxed shoulder line. Our merino raglan cardigan is described as relaxed and slightly oversized, and it comes in one size. Check the garment measurements on its product page before you choose.

Length and fit

Length is the first thing to check. Both long open-front cardigans are described as straight and relaxed, and the shawl-collar cardigan is knee-length. A longer cardigan covers more of the hip, so think about what you wear underneath. The size guide shows how to compare your measurements with ours.

Fibre and warmth

Pure new wool is wool that has not been reclaimed from old knitwear. Merino is finer than most wool, and neither fibre is automatically warmer. Warmth depends on the yarn and the knit, so compare the material line on each page.

Sizing and care

Use our size guide to compare your measurements with ours. Hand wash your cardigan in cool water with a mild wool detergent, lay it flat to dry, and store it folded. Our guide on washing wool sweaters has the full steps.

Which one suits you

Choose the open-front cardigan for a simple layer over dresses and shirts, the shawl-collar cardigan for a longer, softer line, and the raglan for an everyday fit you can wear with jeans or trousers.
