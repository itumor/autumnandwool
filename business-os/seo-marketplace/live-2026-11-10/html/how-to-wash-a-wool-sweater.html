How to wash a wool sweater

Wool washes best when you do it gently and not too often. The steps below use the care instructions on our product pages and FAQ, so they match the pieces you buy from us. If a label says otherwise, follow the label.

Before you start

Check the care line on the product page first. Our sweaters are merino wool, lambswool or pure new wool, depending on the style. Fill a basin with cool water, and add a mild wool detergent. Do not use hot water, because heat and rubbing can make wool felt.

Wash it by hand

Put the sweater in the water and press it gently so the water reaches the fibres. Keep the movement slow and light. Lift it out without wringing or twisting. Wringing stretches the knit and can distort the shape, so support the weight of the wet sweater with both hands.

Dry it flat

Lay the sweater flat on a clean towel. Roll the towel gently to take out some water, then reshape the sweater by hand. Leave it to dry flat, away from radiators and direct heat. Do not tumble dry.

Store it folded

Store the sweater folded, not hung, because hanging can stretch the shoulders. Our FAQ suggests breathable bags with cedar or lavender to help keep moths away.

Oversized and layered pieces

Oversized styles follow the same steps. Because they have more fabric, support the whole piece while it is wet, so it keeps its shape. Relaxed fits need the same gentle handling.

Small problems

A small hole or a dropped stitch is easier to fix when it is caught early. If you are unsure what to do, ask us through the contact page before you wash the piece again.

What to avoid

Avoid bleach and strong detergents, which can damage the fibres. Do not hang a wet sweater, leave it in direct sun, or dry it on a heater. Do not iron over the knit.
