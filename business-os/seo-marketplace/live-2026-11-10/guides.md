<div class="aw aw-seo-page">
<h1>How to wash a wool sweater</h1>
<p>Wool washes best when you do it gently and not too often. The steps below use the care instructions on our product pages and FAQ, so they match the pieces you buy from us. If a label says otherwise, follow the label.</p>
<h2>Before you start</h2>
<p>Check the care line on the product page first. Our sweaters are merino wool, lambswool or pure new wool, depending on the style. Fill a basin with cool water, and add a mild wool detergent. Do not use hot water, because heat and rubbing can make wool felt.</p>
<h2>Wash it by hand</h2>
<p>Put the sweater in the water and press it gently so the water reaches the fibres. Keep the movement slow and light. Lift it out without wringing or twisting. Wringing stretches the knit and can distort the shape, so support the weight of the wet sweater with both hands.</p>
<h2>Dry it flat</h2>
<p>Lay the sweater flat on a clean towel. Roll the towel gently to take out some water, then reshape the sweater by hand. Leave it to dry flat, away from radiators and direct heat. Do not tumble dry.</p>
<h2>Store it folded</h2>
<p>Store the sweater folded, not hung, because hanging can stretch the shoulders. Our <a href="/faq">FAQ</a> suggests breathable bags with cedar or lavender to help keep moths away.</p>
<h2>Oversized and layered pieces</h2>
<p>Oversized styles follow the same steps. Because they have more fabric, support the whole piece while it is wet, so it keeps its shape. Relaxed fits need the same gentle handling.</p>
<h2>Small problems</h2>
<p>A small hole or a dropped stitch is easier to fix when it is caught early. If you are unsure what to do, ask us through the contact page before you wash the piece again.</p>
<h2>What to avoid</h2>
<p>Avoid bleach and strong detergents, which can damage the fibres. Do not hang a wet sweater, leave it in direct sun, or dry it on a heater. Do not iron over the knit.</p>
<h2>Where to go next</h2>
<p>Browse the <a href="/wool-sweaters">wool sweaters</a> range to see every style. Each product page also has its own care line, so check it before you wash. For a fine merino knit, see the <a href="https://www.autumnandwool.com/shop/p/p/merino-wool-turtleneck-sweater-womens-knit-pullover-soft-warm-lightweight-minimalist-sustainable-knitwear-sizes-sl-12-679428">Lightweight Merino Turtleneck</a>.</p>

<div class="aw aw-seo-page">
<h1>Merino or lambswool: which should you choose?</h1>
<p>Merino and lambswool are both soft, warm wool fibres, and both are used in our knitwear. They differ in where the fibre comes from and how the yarn feels. This guide explains the difference in general terms, then shows which of our pieces use each fibre.</p>
<h2>What merino is</h2>
<p>Merino is a breed of sheep, and merino wool is the fleece from those sheep. Merino fibres are generally finer than the wool of many other breeds, so merino knits often feel smooth against the skin. It is a common choice for next-to-skin layers.</p>
<h2>What lambswool is</h2>
<p>Lambswool is the fleece from young sheep, usually taken at their first shearing. Because the fibres come from young animals, they are generally softer and more springy than wool from adult sheep. Lambswool knits tend to feel light and full of loft.</p>
<h2>Feel, fineness and drape</h2>
<p>Merino is usually the finer of the two, so it tends to feel close and smooth. Lambswool has more loft, so a lambswool knit can feel springier and fuller. Both fibres drape well. The stitch and the yarn weight matter as much as the fibre, so read the description on each product page.</p>
<h2>Warmth and weight</h2>
<p>Neither fibre is automatically warmer. Warmth depends on the thickness of the yarn and how tightly the piece is knitted. A fine merino knit can feel as warm as a heavier lambswool piece if the yarn is dense. Compare the material line and the fit notes before you decide.</p>
<h2>Care</h2>
<p>Both need the same gentle care. Wash by hand in cool water with a mild wool detergent, do not wring, and dry flat. Our <a href="/guides/how-to-wash-a-wool-sweater">guide on how to wash a wool sweater</a> covers each step.</p>
<h2>The fibres in our range</h2>
<p>Our merino pieces list merino wool on their product pages, and our lambswool pieces list 100% lambswool, which is also pure new wool. For example, the <a href="https://www.autumnandwool.com/shop/p/p/merino-wool-turtleneck-sweater-womens-knit-pullover-soft-warm-lightweight-minimalist-sustainable-knitwear-sizes-sl-12-679428">Lightweight Merino Turtleneck</a> is 100% merino wool, and the <a href="https://www.autumnandwool.com/shop/p/p/hand-knitted-lambswool-sweater-womens-grey-knit-jumper">Women's Classic Lambswool Crew Sweater</a> is 100% lambswool. Check the material line on the product you are looking at, because it is the source of truth.</p>
<h2>Which one to choose</h2>
<p>Choose merino if you want a finer, smoother feel. Choose lambswool if you want a softer, springier knit. Both are good everyday choices, so start with the style you like, then check the fibre. You can browse the full range on our <a href="/wool-sweaters">wool sweaters</a> page.</p>
