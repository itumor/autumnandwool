## How to wash a wool sweater
By Kristine, designer and maker at Autumn & Wool · Updated 10 November 2026
Wash a wool sweater by hand in cool water with a mild wool detergent. Do not use hot water, do not wring it, and do not tumble dry. Support the wet weight of the knit, lay it flat on a clean towel to dry, and keep it away from radiators and direct heat. Store it folded rather than hung.
### What do you need before you start?
Check the care line on the product page first, because it is the source of truth. Our sweaters are merino wool, lambswool or pure new wool, depending on the style. Fill a basin with cool water and add a mild wool detergent. Do not use hot water, because heat and rubbing can make wool felt.
### How do you hand wash a wool sweater?
Put the sweater in the water and press it gently so the water reaches the fibres. Keep the movement slow and light. Lift it out without wringing or twisting. Wringing stretches the knit and can distort the shape, so support the weight of the wet sweater with both hands.
Oversized styles follow the same steps. Because they have more fabric, support the whole piece while it is wet so it keeps its shape. Relaxed fits need the same gentle handling.
### How do you dry a wool sweater?
Lay the sweater flat on a clean towel. Roll the towel gently to take out some water, then reshape the sweater by hand. Leave it to dry flat, away from radiators and direct heat. Do not tumble dry.
### How do you store a wool sweater?
Store the sweater folded, not hung, because hanging can stretch the shoulders. Our FAQ suggests breathable bags with cedar or lavender to help keep moths away.
### What should you do and avoid?
Step Do Avoid
Water and detergent Use cool water and a mild wool detergent Hot water, bleach and strong detergents
Washing Press gently and keep the movement slow and light Rubbing the knit
Lifting out Support the wet sweater with both hands Wringing or twisting
Drying Lay it flat on a clean towel and reshape it by hand Tumble drying, hanging it wet, direct sun, or drying it on a heater
Storing Store it folded Hanging
Do not iron over the knit. A small hole or a dropped stitch is easier to fix when it is caught early. If you are unsure what to do, ask us through the contact page before you wash the piece again.

## Merino or lambswool: which should you choose?
By Kristine, designer and maker at Autumn & Wool · Updated 10 November 2026
Merino wool is fleece from merino sheep, and it is generally finer, so merino knits often feel smooth against the skin. Lambswool is fleece from young sheep, usually taken at their first shearing, and it is generally softer and more springy. Neither fibre is automatically warmer, because yarn weight and knit decide warmth.
### What are merino and lambswool?
Merino is a breed of sheep, and merino wool is the fleece from those sheep. Merino fibres are generally finer than the wool of many other breeds, so merino knits often feel smooth against the skin. It is a common choice for next-to-skin layers.
Lambswool is the fleece from young sheep, usually taken at their first shearing. Because the fibres come from young animals, they are generally softer and more springy than wool from adult sheep. Lambswool knits tend to feel light and full of loft.
### Which feels softer, merino or lambswool?
Merino is usually the finer of the two, so it tends to feel close and smooth. Lambswool has more loft, so a lambswool knit can feel springier and fuller. Both fibres drape well. The stitch and the yarn weight matter as much as the fibre, so read the description on each product page.
### Is merino or lambswool warmer?
Neither fibre is automatically warmer. Warmth depends on the thickness of the yarn and how tightly the piece is knitted. A fine merino knit can feel as warm as a heavier lambswool piece if the yarn is dense. Compare the material line and the fit notes before you decide.
### How do you care for merino and lambswool?
Both need the same gentle care. Wash by hand in cool water with a mild wool detergent, do not wring, and dry flat. Our guide on how to wash a wool sweater covers each step.
### Which fibre should you choose?

Choose merino if you want a finer, smoother feel. Choose lambswool if you want a softer, springier knit. Both are good everyday choices, so start with the style you like, then check the fibre.
Our merino pieces list merino wool on their product pages, and our lambswool pieces list 100% lambswool, which is also pure new wool. ## Oversized fit guide
By Kristine, designer and maker at Autumn & Wool · Updated 10 November 2026
Oversized and relaxed knits are cut wider and longer than close-fitting knitwear, so they sit away from the body. Choose a size by your body measurements from our size guide, then check the garment width and length on the product page. Oversized pieces are often one size, so compare the garment width with your chest.
### What do relaxed and oversized mean?
Our size guide lists Close-fitting, Relaxed and Oversized fits, plus One size. Relaxed knits have room for layering. Oversized pieces are roomy and made for layering. Each product page gives its own fit note, such as 'Relaxed' or 'Oversized; roomy and easy to layer'. The Lightweight Merino Turtleneck is described as comfortable and close to the body, so check the fit before you choose.
### How do you measure for an oversized sweater?
Measure your chest, waist and hips over thin, fitted clothing, with the tape snug but comfortable. Match your numbers to the body size chart below. Sizes S to XL convert to EU 36 to 42, UK 8 to 14 and US 6 to 12. For example, size S is 84 to 90 cm around the chest, 62 to 70 cm at the waist and 90 to 96 cm at the hips. Size M is 90 to 96 cm at the chest.
Size EU · UK · US Chest (cm) Waist (cm) Hips (cm)
S EU 36 · UK 8 · US 6 84–90 62–70 90–96
M EU 38 · UK 10 · US 8 90–96 70–78 96–104
L EU 40 · UK 12 · US 10 96–104 78–88 104–112
XL EU 42 · UK 14 · US 12 104–110 88–100 112–118
The chart is a general guide. The EU, UK and US sizes match the labels on product pages. The full size guide explains how to measure each area, and it shows how to compare your measurements with the garment measurements.
### How do you compare the garment measurements?
Body measurements are only part of the picture. Oversized pieces are cut wider than the body, so compare the garment measurements on the product page with your chest. Double the garment width to get the full circumference, then compare it with your chest. The difference is the ease, the extra room in the piece. The size guide gives an example: a Pure Wool Side-Slit Sweater in size S measures 50 cm flat, which is about 100 cm (39.4 in) around.
For a close look at the numbers, see the Distressed Oversized Wool Raglan Sweater , which measures 57 cm wide, and the Oversized Merino Turtleneck, Dusty Pink , which measures 53 cm wide.
### Which pieces come in one size?
Vests, some cardigans and oversized pieces are often one size. The size guide advises that you compare the garment width with your chest. Our sweater vests are often one size. The Merino Wool Raglan Cardigan comes in one size and is described as relaxed and slightly oversized. Other pieces list sizes from S to XL, or S/M and L/XL, so check the sizes on the product page before you order.
### Does layering change the fit?
Oversized knits are made for layering, so think about what goes underneath. A sweater vest over a shirt gives a different shape from a loose knit over a fitted top. Our beige melange vest is described as easy to layer over shirts and dresses.
Still unsure? Read the product page first, then send us a question through our contact page . We reply within 1 to 2 business days.

## How to choose a wool cardigan
By Kristine, designer and maker at Autumn & Wool · Updated 10 November 2026
Choose a wool cardigan by style, length and fibre. Our open-front cardigans are straight-cut, the long shawl-collar cardigans have a draped collar, and the merino raglan cardigan has a relaxed shoulder line and comes in one size. Compare the garment measurements and the material line on each product page before you order.
### Which styles do we sell?
We sell three styles of wool cardigan. The open-front cardigan with side slits is straight-cut and relaxed. The two long shawl-collar cardigans are open-front with a draped collar, and one is knee-length. The merino raglan cardigan comes in black, brown or blue.

### Open front or shawl collar?
A shawl collar drapes softly at the neck and front. The open-front cardigan with side slits is straight-cut and has ribbed panels at the sides, so it has a simpler line. Both are 100% pure new wool, as their product pages state, so the choice comes down to the shape you prefer.
### Is a raglan cardigan easy to wear?
A raglan sleeve runs from the underarm up to the neck, which gives a relaxed shoulder line. Our merino raglan cardigan is 100% merino wool and described as relaxed and slightly oversized. It comes in one size, with a listed width of 61 cm, so check the garment measurements on its product page before you choose.
### How long should a cardigan be?
Length is the first thing to check. The long shawl-collar cardigans measure 83 to 86 cm in garment length from S to XL, and the open-front cardigan with side slits measures 70 cm. A longer cardigan covers more of the hip, so think about what you wear underneath. Use our size guide to compare your measurements with ours.
### Which fibre and care suit a cardigan?
Pure new wool is wool that has not been reclaimed from old knitwear. Merino is finer than most wool, and neither fibre is automatically warmer. Warmth depends on the yarn and the knit, so compare the material line on each page. Hand wash your cardigan gently in cool water with a mild wool detergent, do not wring it, and lay it flat to dry.
